How the screwworm works

Four stages. One cycle. It starts with a wound no bigger than a tick bite.

Stage 1

Eggs laid in wounds

Female fly detects any open wound — a tick bite, branding mark, even a navel on a newborn calf — and lays 200–400 eggs directly into living flesh.

200–400 eggs per wound · hatches in 12–24 hrs

Stage 2

Larvae burrow inward

Maggots feed on living tissue and release enzymes that liquefy muscle. They screw deeper with each molt, making the wound grow rapidly.

Left untreated: death in 7–14 days

Stage 3

Pupae drop to soil

Mature larvae exit the wound, fall to the ground, and pupate in the soil for 7 days. Cool soil slows them; warm Southern soil accelerates the cycle year-round.

7-day pupation through three larval instars

Stage 4

Adult fly emerges

A new fly emerges in days, mates once, and a single female can repeat the cycle — finding new hosts across miles of ranchland before dying.

One female: hundreds of new larvae

The supply pipeline problem

Supply shock Demand pressure The result

Drought-driven herd liquidation

2020–23 Southwest droughts forced ranchers to sell cattle they couldn’t feed. The New World Screwworm landed on an already-depleted supply.

The US herd shrank to 87.2M head — a 75-year low

Mexico cattle import ban

In Nov 2024, the first Mexican case was detected in southern Mexico. The USDA immediately suspended all live cattle, horse, and bison imports from Mexico.

Approx. 1.2M feeder cattle/yr supply cut off

Cattle can’t be rebuilt quickly

Unlike pork or poultry, beef cattle take 2–3 years from birth to slaughter. A rancher who sells her breeding stock in 2023 can’t replenish the market before 2026 at the earliest — and screwworm keeps pushing the loss forward.

Birth
Slaughter · 2–3 yrs

Per capita consumption holds

Americans are historically price-inelastic on beef. Even at $9.64/lb, demand hasn’t collapsed. Shoppers shifted to cheaper cuts but total volume held, keeping upward pressure on.

Export markets compete for US beef

Japan, South Korea, and China remain the top three buyers of US beef — together taking over 60% of all exports. Even as export volumes dip, billions of pounds leave US supply every year.

What is being done

The weapon is the fly itself: sterile males. A female mates once — with a sterile male, no offspring.

Sterile flies released

Panama facility ships 100M sterile male flies to Texas weekly. Females mate once — with a sterile male, no offspring. The same technique eradicated screwworm from the US in the 1960s.
